§1.14(i) Fourier Transform

Keywords:

Fourier transform , convergence , definitions

Notes:

See Titchmarsh (1986a, pp. 3–15, 42, 50–59).

Referenced by:

§1.15(v) , (1.16.29) , Erratum (V1.2.0) §1.14(i)

Addition (effective with 1.2.0):

Equation ( 1.14.7_5 ) was added.

Notational Addition (effective with 1.0.15):

A general notation for the Fourier transform of functions of a single variable was not defined in the DLMF. In this release we provide such a definition in ( 1.14.1 ).

See also:

Annotations for §1.14 and Ch.1

The Fourier transform of a real- or complex-valued function \(f(t)\) is defined by

\[ \mathscr{F}\left(f\right)\left(x\right)=\mathscr{F}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}f(t){\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}xt}\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.1} \]

(Some references replace \(\mathrm{i}xt\) by \(-\mathrm{i}xt\) ). The same notation \(\mathscr{F}\) is used for Fourier transforms of functions of several variables and for Fourier transforms of distributions; see § 1.16(vii) .

In this subsection we let \(F(x)=\mathscr{F}\left(f\right)\left(x\right)\) .

If \(f(t)\) is absolutely integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) , then \(F(x)\) is continuous, \(F(x)\to 0\) as \(x\to\pm\infty\) , and

\[ \left|F(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\left|f(t)\right|\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.2} \]

Inversion

Keywords:

Fourier transform , inversion

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(i) , §1.14 and Ch.1

Suppose that \(f(t)\) is absolutely integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) and of bounded variation in a neighborhood of \(t=u\) (§ 1.4(v) ). Then

\[ \tfrac{1}{2}(f(u+)+f(u-))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\fint^{\infty}_{-\infty}F(x){\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}xu}\,\mathrm{d}x, \tag{1.14.3} \]

where the last integral denotes the Cauchy principal value ( 1.4.25 ).

In many applications \(f(t)\) is absolutely integrable and \(f^{\prime}(t)\) is continuous on \((-\infty,\infty)\) . Then

\[ f(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}F(x){\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}xt}\,\mathrm{d}x. \tag{1.14.4} \]

Convolution

Keywords:

Fourier transform , convolution

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(i) , §1.14 and Ch.1

For Fourier transforms, the convolution \((f*g)(t)\) of two functions \(f(t)\) and \(g(t)\) defined on \((-\infty,\infty)\) is given by

\[ (f*g)(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}f(t-s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{1.14.5} \]

If \(f(t)\) and \(g(t)\) are absolutely integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) , then so is \((f*g)(t)\) , and its Fourier transform is \(F(x)G(x)\) , where \(G(x)\) is the Fourier transform of \(g(t)\) .

Parseval’s Formula

Keywords:

Fourier transform , Parseval’s formula

Modification (effective with 1.2.0):

In the first sentence of this paragraph “absolutely integrable” was replaced with “absolutely and square integrable”.

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(i) , §1.14 and Ch.1

Suppose \(f(t)\) and \(g(t)\) are absolutely and square integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) , then

\[ (f*g)(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}F(x)G(x){\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tx}\,\mathrm{d}x, \tag{1.14.6} \]

\[ \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}F(x)G(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}f(t)g(-t)\,\mathrm{d}t, \tag{1.14.7} \]

\[ \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}F(x)\overline{G(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}f(t)\overline{g(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t, \tag{1.14.7_5} \]

\[ \int^{\infty}_{-\infty}{\left|F(x)\right|}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}{\left|f(t)\right|}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.8} \]

( 1.14.7_5 ) and ( 1.14.8 ) are Parseval’s formulas .

Uniqueness

Keywords:

Fourier transform , uniqueness

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(i) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(f(t)\) and \(g(t)\) are continuous and absolutely integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) , and \(F(x)=G(x)\) for all \(x\) , then \(f(t)=g(t)\) for all \(t\) .

§1.14(iii) Laplace Transform

Keywords:

Laplace transform , definition , notation

Notes:

See Schiff (1999, pp. 12–57, 91–93, 151–157).

Referenced by:

§18.17(i) , §2.4(ii) , §3.11(iv) , §3.5(viii) , §3.5(ix)

See also:

Annotations for §1.14 and Ch.1

Suppose \(f(t)\) is a real- or complex-valued function and \(s\) is a real or complex parameter. The Laplace transform of \(f\) is defined by

\[ \mathscr{L}\left(f\right)\left(s\right)=\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=\int^{\infty}_{0}{\mathrm{e}}^{-st}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.17} \]

Convergence and Analyticity

Keywords:

Laplace transform , analyticity , convergence , exponential growth

Modifications (effective with 1.0.15):

The wording of the sentence above ( 1.14.18 ) was modified for clarity.

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(iii) , §1.14 and Ch.1

Assume that \(f(t)\) is piecewise continuous on \([0,\infty)\) and of exponential growth , that is, constants \(M\) and \(\alpha\) exist such that

\[ \left|f(t)\right|\leq M{\mathrm{e}}^{\alpha t}, \tag{1.14.18} \]

Then \(\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\) is an analytic function of \(s\) for \(\Re s>\alpha\) . Moreover,

\[ \mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\to 0, \tag{1.14.19} \]

Throughout the remainder of this subsection we assume ( 1.14.18 ) is satisfied and \(\Re s>\alpha\) .

Inversion

Keywords:

Laplace transform , inversion

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(iii) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(f(t)\) is continuous and \(f^{\prime}(t)\) is piecewise continuous on \([0,\infty)\) , then

\[ f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\int^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}T}_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}T}{\mathrm{e}}^{ts}\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\,\mathrm{d}s, \tag{1.14.20} \]

Moreover, if \(\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=O\left(s^{-K}\right)\) in some half-plane \(\Re s\geq\gamma\) and \(K>1\) , then ( 1.14.20 ) holds for \(\sigma>\gamma\) .

Translation

Keywords:

Laplace transform , translation

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(iii) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(\Re s>\max(\Re(a+\alpha),\alpha)\) , then

\[ \mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s-a\right)=\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f_{a}\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right), \tag{1.14.21} \]

where \(f_{a}(t)={\mathrm{e}}^{at}f(t)\) . Also, if \(a\geq 0\) then

\[ \mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f_{a}^{+}\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)={\mathrm{e}}^{-as}\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right), \tag{1.14.22} \]

where \(f^{+}_{a}(t)=H\left(t-a\right)f(t-a)\) and \(H\) is the Heaviside function; see ( 1.16.13 ).

Differentiation and Integration

Keywords:

Laplace transform , differentiation , integration

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(iii) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(f(t)\) is piecewise continuous, then

\[ {(-1)}^{n}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{n}}{{\mathrm{d}s}^{n}}\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f_{n}\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right), \tag{1.14.23} \]

where \(f_{n}(t)=t^{n}f(t)\) . If also \(\lim_{t\to 0+}f(t)/t\) exists, then

\[ \int^{\infty}_{s}\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(u\right)\,\mathrm{d}u=\mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f_{-1}\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right), \tag{1.14.24} \]

where \(f_{-1}(t)=\frac{f(t)}{t}\) .

Periodic Functions

Keywords:

Laplace transform , of periodic functions

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(iii) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(a>0\) and \(f(t+a)=f(t)\) for \(t>0\) , then

\[ \mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-as}}\int^{a}_{0}{\mathrm{e}}^{-st}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.25} \]

Alternatively if \(f(t+a)=-f(t)\) for \(t>0\) , then

\[ \mathscr{L}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=\frac{1}{1+{\mathrm{e}}^{-as}}\int^{a}_{0}{\mathrm{e}}^{-st}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.26} \]

Derivatives

Keywords:

Laplace transform , derivatives

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(iii) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(f(t)\) is continuous on \([0,\infty)\) and \(f^{\prime}(t)\) is piecewise continuous on \((0,\infty)\) , then

\[ \mathscr{L}\left(f^{\prime}\right)\left(s\right)=s\mathscr{L}\left(f\right)\left(s\right)-f(0+). \tag{1.14.27} \]

If \(f(t)\) and \(f^{\prime}(t)\) are piecewise continuous on \([0,\infty)\) with discontinuities at ( \(0=\) ) \(t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}\) , then

\[ \mathscr{L}\left(f^{\prime}\right)\left(s\right)=s\mathscr{L}\left(f\right)\left(s\right)-f(0+)-\sum^{n}_{k=1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-st_{k}}(f(t_{k}+)-f(t_{k}-)). \tag{1.14.28} \]

Next, assume \(f(t)\) , \(f^{\prime}(t)\) , \(\dots\) , \(f^{(n-1)}(t)\) are continuous and each satisfies ( 1.14.18 ). Also assume that \(f^{(n)}(t)\) is piecewise continuous on \([0,\infty)\) . Then

\[ \mathscr{L}\left(f^{(n)}\right)\left(s\right)=s^{n}\mathscr{L}\left(f\right)\left(s\right)-s^{n-1}f(0+)-s^{n-2}f^{\prime}(0+)-\dots-f^{(n-1)}(0+). \tag{1.14.29} \]

For Laplace transforms, the convolution of two functions \(f(t)\) and \(g(t)\) , defined on \([0,\infty)\) , is

\[ (f*g)(t)=\int^{t}_{0}f(u)g(t-u)\,\mathrm{d}u. \tag{1.14.30} \]

If \(f(t)\) and \(g(t)\) are piecewise continuous, then

\[ \mathscr{L}\left(f*g\right)=\mathscr{L}\left(f\right)\mathscr{L}\left(g\right). \tag{1.14.31} \]

§1.14(iv) Mellin Transform

Keywords:

Mellin transform , analyticity , convergence , definition , notation

Notes:

See Paris and Kaminski (2001, pp. 79–89), Titchmarsh (1986a, pp. 51–53, 60), and Wong (1989, pp. 147–152).

Referenced by:

§2.5(i) , §3.5(ix) , §7.7(ii)

See also:

The Mellin transform of a real- or complex-valued function \(f(x)\) is defined by

\[ \mathscr{M}\left(f\right)\left(s\right)=\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=\int^{\infty}_{0}x^{s-1}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x. \tag{1.14.32} \]

If \(x^{\sigma-1}f(x)\) is integrable on \((0,\infty)\) for all \(\sigma\) in \(a<\sigma<b\) , then the integral ( 1.14.32 ) converges and \(\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\) is an analytic function of \(s\) in the vertical strip \(a<\Re s<b\) . Moreover, for \(a<\sigma<b\) ,

\[ \lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t\right)=0. \tag{1.14.33} \]

Note: If \(f(x)\) is continuous and \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are real numbers such that \(f(x)=O\left(x^{\alpha}\right)\) as \(x\to 0+\) and \(f(x)=O\left(x^{\beta}\right)\) as \(x\to\infty\) , then \(x^{\sigma-1}f(x)\) is integrable on \((0,\infty)\) for all \(\sigma\in(-\alpha,-\beta)\) .

Inversion

Suppose the integral ( 1.14.32 ) is absolutely convergent on the line \(\Re s=\sigma\) and \(f(x)\) is of bounded variation in a neighborhood of \(x=u\) . Then

\[ \tfrac{1}{2}(f(u+)+f(u-))=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\lim_{T\to\infty}\int^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}T}_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}T}u^{-s}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{1.14.34} \]

If \(f(x)\) is continuous on \((0,\infty)\) and \(\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t\right)\) is integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) , then

\[ f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}x^{-s}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{1.14.35} \]

Suppose \(x^{-\sigma}f(x)\) and \(x^{\sigma-1}g(x)\) are absolutely integrable on \((0,\infty)\) and either \(\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}g\mspace{3.0mu}\left(\sigma+\mathrm{i}t\right)\) or \(\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(1-\sigma-\mathrm{i}t\right)\) is absolutely integrable on \((-\infty,\infty)\) . Then for \(y>0\) ,

\[ \int^{\infty}_{0}f(x)g(yx)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\int^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}y^{-s}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(1-s\right)\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}g\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\,\mathrm{d}s, \tag{1.14.36} \]

\[ \int^{\infty}_{0}f(x)g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\int^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(1-s\right)\mathscr{M}\mspace{-3.0mu}g\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\,\mathrm{d}s. \tag{1.14.37} \]

Let

§1.14(vi) Stieltjes Transform

Keywords:

Stieltjes transform , analyticity , convergence , definition , derivatives

Notes:

See Widder (1941, pp. 325–328, 340–341).

See also:

Annotations for §1.14 and Ch.1

The Stieltjes transform of a real-valued function \(f(t)\) is defined by

\[ \mathcal{S}\left(f\right)\left(s\right)=\mathcal{S}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{f(t)}{s+t}\,\mathrm{d}t. \tag{1.14.47} \]

Sufficient conditions for the integral to converge are that \(s\) is a positive real number, and \(f(t)=O\left(t^{-\delta}\right)\) as \(t\to\infty\) , where \(\delta>0\) .

If the integral converges, then it converges uniformly in any compact domain in the complex \(s\) -plane not containing any point of the interval \((-\infty,0]\) . In this case, \(\mathcal{S}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)\) represents an analytic function in the \(s\) -plane cut along the negative real axis, and

\[ \frac{{\mathrm{d}}^{m}}{{\mathrm{d}s}^{m}}\mathcal{S}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(s\right)=(-1)^{m}m!\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{(s+t)^{m+1}}, \tag{1.14.48} \]

Inversion

Keywords:

Stieltjes transform , inversion

See also:

Annotations for §1.14(vi) , §1.14 and Ch.1

If \(f(t)\) is absolutely integrable on \([0,R]\) for every finite \(R\) , and the integral ( 1.14.47 ) converges, then

\[ \lim_{t\to 0+}\frac{\mathcal{S}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(-\sigma-\mathrm{i}t\right)-\mathcal{S}\mspace{-3.0mu}f\mspace{3.0mu}\left(-\sigma+\mathrm{i}t\right)}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(f(\sigma+)+f(\sigma-)), \tag{1.14.49} \]

for all values of the positive constant \(\sigma\) for which the right-hand side exists.
